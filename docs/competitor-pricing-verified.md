## What is honestly true about Flas, and what is not

Write from this. Overclaiming here is what gets a comparison page cited as
misleading.

**True and defensible:**

- Flas is the cheapest hosted WhatsApp CRM in this comparison at any team size
  above one agent.
- Flas does not charge per seat. Wati, Respond.io, Zoko (above Starter) and
  Chatwoot all do.
- The gap widens with team size rather than narrowing — the opposite of most
  "cheap tier" products.
- Every feature is on every plan; there is no feature-gated upgrade path.

**Must be conceded, because it is true:**

- **Chatwoot is cheaper for a single agent** ($19 vs $20, and $0 for two agents
  on the Hacker plan). It only becomes expensive as the team grows.
- **Zoko Starter also has unlimited agents** at $49.99, so the "no per-seat
  pricing" argument does not distinguish Flas from Zoko — only the price does.
- **Chatwoot is open source and self-hostable.** If a business wants to own the
  deployment, that is a real advantage Flas does not offer.
- **Respond.io and Wati are established products** with far more integrations,
  larger support organisations and years of track record. Flas is new.
- **Twilio has no monthly fee at all.** For a business sending a low volume
  through its own software, pay-as-you-go is genuinely cheaper — Twilio is
  infrastructure, not a CRM, and the comparison is not like for like.

**Never claim:**

- That any competitor is "hiding" fees. Wati, Respond.io and Zoko all state
  plainly that Meta's conversation charges are separate. So does Flas.
- Any Interakt or AiSensy price, until the LOW CONFIDENCE items above are
  rechecked by hand.
- Any specific Meta per-message rate. It varies by country and Meta revises it.

## One point every comparison post should make

All of these vendors, Flas included, bill **only for the software**. Meta bills
per conversation or per message separately, at rates that vary by country. A
reader comparing $20 against $119 is comparing subscription cost only — their
Meta spend will be similar whichever they choose, and on high volume it will
dwarf the subscription.

Saying this plainly builds more trust than hiding it, and it is the single most
common thing buyers get wrong.
